The movement skills behind play

When we run, hop, throw or dodge, we use basic movement skills. Practising them through play helps children grow confident in their bodies, and helps adults stay mobile and coordinated as they age.

Balance

Staying steady while standing, moving or changing direction, such as walking along a line or standing on one leg.

Coordination

Making eyes, hands and feet work together, such as catching, kicking or dribbling a ball.

Agility

Changing direction quickly and smoothly, such as in tag games and zig-zag runs.

Strength and power

Pushing, pulling, jumping and climbing using the large muscles of the body.

Flexibility

Reaching, bending and twisting comfortably through a full range of motion.

Rhythm and timing

Moving in time with music or a partner, such as clapping patterns and dance steps.

Good to know: children learn movement best through variety. Mixing running, jumping, throwing, climbing and balancing is better than repeating one activity.

Easy movement games to try today

These games need little or no equipment and can be played indoors or outdoors in a safe, clear space.

GameHow it worksSkills used
Freeze danceDance while music plays and freeze when it stopsBalance, rhythm, listening
Animal walksMove like a frog, crab, bear or snake across the roomStrength, coordination
Obstacle courseCrawl under chairs, step over cushions, balance on a line of tapeAgility, balance, planning
Balloon keep-upKeep a balloon in the air without letting it touch the floorEye-hand coordination, teamwork
Shadow tagTry to step on another player's shadow outdoorsAgility, speed
Mirror meOne person moves slowly and a partner copies exactlyControl, focus, creativity

Make any game better

  1. Keep rules short so that play starts within a minute.
  2. Let players change the rules, which builds creativity.
  3. Adjust the difficulty up or down so that everyone feels capable.
  4. End while it is still fun, then ask players to choose the next game.

Dance and rhythm at home

Dance is one of the most enjoyable ways to move. It builds rhythm, memory and coordination, lifts your mood and needs only a little floor space. You can follow a free online class, a family routine or just move to your favourite music.

Start small

  • Clear a space and wear comfortable clothes and non-slip shoes or socks with grip.
  • Begin with a five-minute warm-up of marching, shoulder rolls and gentle hip circles.
  • Learn one simple step at a time, then link steps together.
  • Choose music with a steady beat that makes you want to move.

Traditional and folk styles

Many regions have lively folk dances such as bhangra, garba and dandiya, which combine rhythm, group formations and joyful movement. Learning them from a local group or teacher is a wonderful way to stay active and connect with culture.

Active video games: screen and movement together

Some video games use motion sensors, cameras or dance mats so that you must move to play. These are sometimes called active video games or exergames. They can encourage activity on days when going outside is difficult, and they work well for families.

How to use them well

  • Clear furniture and sharp objects from the play area, and keep a safe distance from the screen.
  • Use wrist straps or controller covers if the device recommends them.
  • Check the age rating, and play together at first with children.
  • Treat them as one part of an active week, alongside outdoor play and sport.
  • Take breaks, drink water and stop if you feel dizzy or in pain.

Movement breaks for school, home and work

Sitting for long periods can leave anyone feeling stiff and tired. A one to three-minute movement break every 30 to 60 minutes helps you refresh your body and attention.

BreakWhat to doTime
Stand and stretchReach arms overhead, then gently lean left and right1 min
March on the spotLift knees and swing arms to a steady beat1 min
Desk squatsStand up and sit down slowly 8 to 10 times1 min
Balance challengeStand on one foot, then switch1 min
Shake it outGently shake hands, arms and legs, then roll the shoulders30 sec

Teachers and parents can turn breaks into short games, such as "stretch like a tree" or "walk like a robot", to make them more fun for children.

Tip: set a repeating timer on your phone or computer to remind you. Small and frequent beats rare and long.

Moving safely

  • Check the space for hazards such as wet floors, loose rugs, sharp furniture edges and uneven ground.
  • Warm up gently before energetic play and cool down afterwards.
  • Wear comfortable clothing and suitable footwear.
  • Drink water and rest when tired, especially in hot weather.
  • Supervise young children, and choose activities that suit their age and ability.
  • Stop and seek help if you feel chest pain, dizziness, severe breathlessness or sharp pain.

Frequently asked questions

How much active play do children need?

Health organisations commonly recommend that children get regular physical activity every day, including active play. Exact guidance depends on age and country, so ask a paediatrician or check your national health guidelines.

Can adults benefit from active play?

Yes. Playful movement such as dancing, games and walking with friends can make activity more enjoyable and easier to keep up.

Do I need equipment or a large space?

No. Most movement games can be played in a living room or small yard, using everyday items like cushions, balloons or tape.
